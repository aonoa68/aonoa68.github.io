READ WITH PRIMATE ECOLOGICAL DATA

A Guide to Statistical Thinking

From summarizing data to expressing relationships as equations. Eight ideas, checked one at a time by working with the same data.

ABOUT THIS GUIDE

Written for readers who have never studied statistics, so that reading one page at a time traces the line of reasoning. Every page uses the same primate dataset and nothing else.

376primate species, one row per species
20variables, including body mass, group size, longevity, home range size, and the temperature of the geographic range
8ideas, each with a hands-on panel that works with the data

The data are the primate portion of PanTHERIA (Jones et al. 2009, Ecology 90: 2648), also used in the courses Statistics I and II. Some species lack records for some variables, so the number of species differs from page to page.

  1. LOOKING AT DATA
  2. 01SUMMARIZING DATA

    Does the mean describe a typical species?

    Mean, median, and spread. Which number should summarize skewed data.

  3. 02GRAPHS

    Change one axis, and the data look different

    Histograms, scatter plots, and log scales for data spanning orders of magnitude.

  4. 03CORRELATION AND CAUSATION

    Do longer-lived species range more widely?

    The correlation coefficient, and the third variable behind a relationship.

  5. INFERRING THE WHOLE FROM A PART
  6. 04SAMPLING

    Why one spoonful tells you how the soup tastes

    Samples and the central limit theorem. More data does not fix a biased sample.

  7. 05CONFIDENCE INTERVALS

    What exactly is 95% about a 95% interval?

    Estimating the whole from a part, with a range rather than a single number.

  8. 06HYPOTHESIS TESTS

    When a real difference is “not significant”

    Measuring whether chance could produce a result. p-values and effect size.

  9. PROBABILITY AND EQUATIONS
  10. 07PROBABILITY AND BAYES

    Do large species form large groups, or the other way round?

    The direction of conditional probability, and Bayes’ theorem.

  11. 08REGRESSION

    If body mass is ten times larger, how much larger is the home range?

    Regression lines, reading the slope, and residuals.
